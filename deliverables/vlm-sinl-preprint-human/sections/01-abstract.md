## Abstract

Hallucinations in Large Languange Models is a one of the most pressing limitation of this technology. Especially, in Vision Languange Models, hallucination plague is even more rampant and punitive. To mitigate this, large amount of research is being done, and while there is no straight-forward solution yet found - there are many known symptoms that help to track these hallucinations. Attention Sinks, being one of the most tell-tale signs of hallucination, have been deeply researched and somewhat mitigated to an extent, and it has shown verifiable improvements too. But when it comes to Vision Language Models, Attention Sinks studies are still in a nascent stage, relative to Text Languange Models, where it was found that the very first token was being attended to disproportionately, and was found to have three underlying signatures: Sink ratios (Sinkε1), Value-Norm ratio (v-ratio), and Massive Activation (h-ratio). In this study, we look at the emergence of these three signatures in a Vision Languange Model with 222 million parameters (a SigLIP-B/16 encoder feeding a randomly initialized SmolLM2-135M-architecture decoder) under four training levers: standard softmax attention, output-gated softmax, unnormalized sigmoid attention,and decoder initialization from a pretrained text LM. We observed that across 3 seeds, the four training levers land in different corners in both: v-ratio vs attention concentration & h-ratio vs attention concentration plots. Prior text-only work separates massive activations from attention sinks; we show, for the first time in from-scratch multimodal pretraining, that all three signatures, value-norm drain included, respond independently to ordinary training-time choices.
