Abstract. Attention concentration, reduced value norms, and unusually large activations often co-occur at attention-sink tokens in language models. Whether these signatures remain coupled during vision-language pretraining is unclear. We track attention concentration, value-norm ratio, and a residual-norm proxy for massive activations throughout pretraining of a 222M-parameter vision-language model. Its first token is an image token, with no BOS token. We compare softmax attention, output-gated softmax, and unnormalized sigmoid attention with randomly initialized decoders, plus a pretrained text-decoder control. Across 2-3 seeds per condition, the signatures form distinct profiles: value norms are reduced under softmax and text initialization but amplified under sigmoid attention, despite strong relative attention concentration in the latter. In a separate single-seed 1B-token run with low data repetition, the residual-norm ratio rises from 1.43 to 3.22 while no head's probe-averaged attention to the first token crosses any tested threshold, 0.2, 0.3, or 0.4. Joint tracking during multimodal pretraining thus reveals value-norm drain as a separately varying third signature, extending the attention-versus-activation dissociations reported in text-only models.
