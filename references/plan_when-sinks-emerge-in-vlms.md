# When Attention Sinks Emerge in Vision-Language Models: Feasibility and Scoop Assessment

## TL;DR
- **The proposed study — training small VLMs from scratch with dense checkpointing to map *when* attention sinks emerge during multimodal pretraining — appears genuinely unclaimed as of June 12, 2026 (scoop risk: LOW–MEDIUM).** Every VLM attention-sink paper found analyzes already-trained/frozen models at inference time; none trains from scratch and tracks emergence dynamics across checkpoints in the multimodal setting.
- **The plan is technically and financially feasible.** nanoVLM (222M = SigLIP-B/16 + SmolLM2-135M, ~750 lines pure PyTorch) is the recommended codebase; from-scratch validation fits on the RTX 4070 Super 12GB (with modest batch sizes), and a Gu-style ≤1B replication fits inside the $200 vast.ai budget. Sinks are documented to emerge very early in LM pretraining (~step 1k / first ~1B tokens), enabling cheap, pre-registered early-exit gates.
- **Deadlines are tight but workable:** ICLR 2027 full paper is expected ~late September 2026 (UNVERIFIED — ICLR 2025 was Sep 24, 2024); NeurIPS 2026 (Dec 6–12, Sydney) workshops must notify authors by the mandatory Sep 29, 2026 deadline, with workshop paper submissions typically late-Aug to mid-Sep 2026; TMLR remains rolling.

## Key Findings

### 1. Scoop check — LOW–MEDIUM risk; the emergence-during-VLM-pretraining angle is open
No paper has trained VLMs from scratch with dense checkpointing to map *when/where* attention sinks form during multimodal pretraining, nor analyzed whether attention concentration and value-norm drain decouple during that process. The closest works are all inference-time / frozen-model analyses:

- **arXiv:2510.08510 "To Sink or Not to Sink: Visual Information Pathways in LVLMs"** (Luo, Fan et al., UBC/Vector Institute) — analyzes *frozen* LLaVA-v1.5-7B and other off-the-shelf LVLMs; distinguishes ViT-propagated sinks (high-norm visual tokens, typically 3–5 per image with norms above 100 receiving ~7× more attention) from LLM-emerged sinks. Its Appendix A.4 tracks propagated-ViT-sink hidden dimensions across training iterations and finds that high-value sink dimensions emerge only in the *early fine-tuning (alignment) stage*, not during projector pretraining — **but the ViT is frozen throughout, the LLM is a pretrained backbone (not from-scratch), checkpointing is sparse, it tracks only hidden-dimension magnitudes (not attention concentration), and there is no concentration-vs-value-norm decoupling analysis.** This is the single closest prior work but only grazes the question.
- **arXiv:2604.03316 "When Sinks Help or Hurt: Unified Framework for Attention Sink in LVLMs"** (KAIST/Chung-Ang/TUM, dated April 1, 2026) — categorizes V-sinks (ViT-emerged) vs L-sinks (LLM-emerged in deep layers) and proposes Layer-wise Sink Gating (LSG), trained with the LVLM backbone frozen. "Where sinks originate" refers to *which component/layer*, a static architectural attribution on trained models — **not training-time emergence across checkpoints. No from-scratch training.**
- **arXiv:2503.03321 "See What You Are Told: Visual Attention Sink in LMMs"** (Kang et al.) — purely inference-time; proposes training-free Visual Attention Redistribution (VAR) with "no additional training, models, or inference steps." No emergence study.
- Gu et al.'s methodology (from-scratch pretraining + dense checkpoint tracking of sink emergence) has been applied to **text LMs only** — and recently extended to diffusion LMs (arXiv:2510.15731) and to a coupled "massive-activation / compression-valley / sink" account in text LLMs (arXiv:2510.06477), but **never to the VLM/multimodal setting.**

**Verdict: the gap is genuinely open.** Residual risk is MEDIUM rather than LOW because the field is moving fast (2604.03316 appeared April 2026 and cites 2510.08510), so a competing from-scratch follow-up could surface before August 2026. **Mitigation: pre-register immediately to establish priority.**

### 3. Base codebase — recommend nanoVLM
- **nanoVLM (HuggingFace) — RECOMMENDED.** 222M = SigLIP-B/16-224 (85M) + SmolLM2-135M; entire model + training logic in ~750 lines of pure PyTorch with **no Trainer/accelerate/deepspeed dependencies** (a deliberate design constraint that makes attention-module surgery trivial). Components: `vision_transformer.py` (~150 lines), `language_model.py` (~250), `modality_projection.py` (~50). Per the official README, "Training this for ~6h on a single H100 GPU on ~1.7M samples of the cauldron results in an accuracy of 35.3% on MMStar." Ships with `measure_vram.py`. **Best hackability for inserting the G1 gate, swapping softmax→sigmoid, and adding dense checkpointing.** Note: after June 4, 2025, a new 450M variant became the repo default; the **v0.1 release retains the simpler 222M pipeline** and is the right starting point.
- **TinyLLaVA:** frozen vision encoder + trainable projector + small LM; uses DeepSpeed ZeRO; flexible but heavier, oriented to fine-tuning rather than from-scratch LM training.
- **SmolVLM:** 256M/500M/2.2B; inference VRAM 0.8/1.2/4.9GB; production-oriented and less minimal than nanoVLM.
- **MobileVLM:** MobileLLaMA 1.4B/2.7B trained from scratch (pretrain on CC-595K, fine-tune on LLaVA-Instruct-158K); mobile-optimized, larger than ideal for a 12GB card.
- **Smallest documented sink (any modality):** In text LMs, Gu et al. found attention sink "even in Pythia-14M"; arXiv:2510.06477 shows coupled sink/massive-activation emergence around step 1k in Pythia-410M. **There is no published report of attention sinks documented in a from-scratch-trained ~200M–1B *multimodal* model** — confirming both the feasibility (sinks exist at tiny scale) and the novelty of the proposed measurement.

### 5. Methodology details to mirror
- **Sink metric.** Sink^ε_1 = the fraction of (layer, head) pairs whose *average* attention to the first token exceeds ε. Gu et al. state: "we prefer to select a threshold that is both strict in quantifying attention sink and less sensitive to the token length T. This gives us the selection of ϵ = 0.3. For fair comparisons, we need to fix T when computing the metric, e.g., T = 64." Robustness checks use ε ∈ {0.2, 0.4}.
- **Checkpoint schedule (from the closely analogous arXiv:2510.06477).** Sampled steps **1, 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k, 10k, 20k, 30k, 143k** for Pythia, recording per-layer entropy, BOS sink rate (τ=0.3), and BOS-token norm — all three found to co-emerge around step 1k. Recommend denser early checkpoints (every ~250 steps over steps 0–2k) for the VLM emergence map, then every ~1k.
- **Sigmoid attention.** Replace softmax with **sigmoid without normalization** — scores need not be non-negative or sum to one; Gu et al. report this prevents both attention sinks and massive activations up to 1B parameters (val loss 3.10 vs softmax 3.07).
- **Gu key findings to test in the VLM regime.** Sinks act as **key biases** (store extra attention, contribute little to value computation); sink position is highly correlated with loss function and data distribution (prefix-LM shifts the sink among prefix tokens; shifted-window attention pins it to the absolute first token; smaller window suppresses it); weight decay encourages sinks (up to a point); smaller learning rates delay and weaken sink emergence; instruction tuning has insignificant effect (so emergence is a *pretraining* phenomenon).
- **Qiu G1 gate (arXiv:2505.06708, Qiu, Wang, Zheng et al., Qwen Team; NeurIPS 2025 Oral).** Y′ = Y ⊙ σ(XW_θ): a **head-specific, multiplicative sigmoid gate applied to the SDPA output**, where X is the post-pre-normalization hidden state. **No special initialization for the main G1 gate** (trained as a standard linear layer; only an input-independent *control* variant is zero-initialized — do NOT attribute a "σ≈0.5 bias init" to Qiu; that comes from the later arXiv:2601.15305). **No separate gate learning rate** — the gate trains under the model's global LR schedule (max ~2e-3 for the 15A2B MoE, ~4–4.5e-3 for the 1.7B dense), and a key benefit is that gating *tolerates larger LRs*. First-token attention drop (verbatim, Fig. 2): "an average of 46.7% of attention scores across layers directed towards the first token. Introducing a gate effectively alleviates this, reducing the proportion to 4.8%," and separately "Layer 21 in the baseline model demonstrates a strong attention sink (83% on the first token), which is substantially reduced by the gate (4%)." The "46.7%→~0" figure in the proposal is an imprecise paraphrase — **the correct average is 4.8%**, and 83%→4% is the layer-21-specific value.
- **Pitfalls in tiny-VLM from-scratch training.** The SigLIP vision encoder is conventionally **frozen** during projector alignment; full from-scratch (random-init LM and/or unfrozen ViT) is less standard and may be less stable — but this is precisely the lever that lets you disentangle sinks *inherited from text init* vs *re-formed during alignment* vs *formed fresh*. Data: the cauldron (and FineVision, used for nanoVLM 450M ablations) are accessible.

### 6. Early-exit gate placement (concrete numbers)
Attention sinks emerge **early** in pretraining. Per Gu et al., "attention sink emerges after effective optimization on sufficient training data" — and arXiv:2510.06477 pins the coupled emergence of massive activations, entropy collapse, and sink surge to **around step 1k** in Pythia-410M, persisting thereafter. arXiv:2504.02732 ("Why do LLMs attend to the first token?") shows the sink rate rises **cumulatively** through training and scales with context length, in ~120M-parameter models trained on 5B total tokens. **Translation to the VLM plan:** at a ~1M-token batch, ~1k–2k steps ≈ the **first ~1–2B tokens** is the earliest defensible go/no-go window — a baseline that has formed no measurable sink (Sink^0.3_1 below ~5%) by ~1B tokens is the trigger to stop.

## Details: recommended experimental design
Use **nanoVLM 222M (v0.1 pipeline)** as the workhorse. Insert clean hooks in the attention module for (a) the Qiu G1 sigmoid output gate, (b) softmax→sigmoid-without-normalization, and add dense checkpointing (every ~250 steps over steps 0–2k, then every ~1k). Log per-layer/head Sink^ε_1 at ε∈{0.2,0.3,0.4} with fixed T, plus first-token/sink-token value-vector ℓ2-norm and hidden-state norm, at every checkpoint — this is what lets you test the **decoupling** of attention concentration from value-norm drain, the most distinctive contribution.

**Intervention arms (as planned):** (a) Qiu G1 sigmoid output gate; (b) sigmoid attention without softmax; (c) prefix visual tokens vs **DeepStack-style multi-layer additive injection** (arXiv:2406.04334 — split visual tokens into groups fed into successive LLM layers bottom-to-top, same context length); (d) text-initialized (pretrained SmolLM2) vs full from-scratch (random-init LM).

3. **Stage 2 (July, vast.ai 4090):** Run the 5-arm sweep at ~3–5B tokens each. **Early-exit gate B:** if the sigmoid/G1 arms fail to reduce the sink relative to baseline by ~2B tokens (i.e., arms don't separate), stop before paying for the A100 run.
4. **Stage 3 (Jul 15–Aug 15, A100-80GB):** One ~1B-class scaled confirmation mirroring Gu's setup (5B tokens, ε=0.3, T=64), **only if Stage 2 shows clean arm separation.**
